## Findings

### Fixed in this batch

1. **security** — `daemon.log` was 0644 (world-readable) and names the account and what plays. The Client now creates
   it 0600 (and tightens an old one), `packaging/launchd/install.sh` makes it 0600 before launchd opens it, and the
   Daemon `fchmod`s its own stderr to 0600 whoever started it. Checked: a 0644 file passed as stderr is 0600 a second
   after the Daemon starts.
2. **security** — the Daemon never checked who connected; it relied only on the socket directory's mode, and didn't
   check the directory was its own. On Linux without `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` that directory is under the shared `/tmp`, so
   another local user who created it first (or a symlink in its place) could put their own socket where Clients look.
   Now `bind` refuses a directory that is a symlink or not ours (or whose parent isn't ours or root's), the Daemon
   drops connections from other uids (`peer_cred`), and the Client refuses a Daemon running as another user.
   Test: `the_socket_never_goes_through_a_symlinked_directory`.
3. **crash** — `config::set_value` panicked when a section wasn't a plain table: an inline table (`ui = { design =
   "nebula" }`, valid TOML) or a stray `ui = 5`, then any change from the Settings page. Now an inline table becomes a
   table with its values, anything else gives way to one; a real table is left where it is. Test:
   `a_setting_is_set_whatever_shape_its_section_has`.
4. **resources** — the Catalogue's caches grew for as long as the Daemon ran: every list, artist page, search, lyrics
   and track name looked at. Now bounded: expired entries go first, then the oldest (lists 40, artist pages and
   searches 60, names 2000, lyrics 300). Test: `caches_stay_bounded`.
5. **security (hardening)** — cover downloads were read whole and decoded with the image library's default limits
   (512 MB). Now a cover over 8 MB is refused (the Client's covers and the Daemon's notification cover), and decoding
   is capped at 4096×4096 and 256 MB. Spotify's largest covers are 640 px. (No crash could be shown before: the
   decoder fails on a lying header before allocating; this is a tighter fence, not a fixed bug.) A body without a
   length is now read in chunks and dropped at the cap (#73, test `a_body_without_a_length_stops_at_the_cap`).
6. **crash (hardening)** — the Core Audio tap callback (macOS, the app as Engine) called Rust with nothing to catch a
   panic, which would unwind into Core Audio's thread; now the buffer is dropped instead.
7. **data/annoyance** (found during #62) — a volume set while Soloist restarted was lost, and a volume of exactly 0 was
   never saved (`1ce954b`).
8. **resources** (Linux session, 2026-10-20) — the kitty shared-memory segments were named after `next_id`, the next
   *image* id, which doesn't change within a flush or between a Visual's frames. So every upload after the first in a
   flush was refused (`O_EXCL`) and fell back to zlib + base64 (the cost the frame profile is about), and once frames
   had run 2 s the sweep, unlinking by name, could remove the newest segment before kitty read it (a dropped frame).
   Each segment now has a name of its own (`shm_seq`). Test: `every_shared_memory_upload_gets_a_segment_of_its_own`
   (fails without the fix: 1 of 3 uploads through shared memory).
9. **polish** (owner report) — the Terminal design's osc kept its full height for ~3 s after a pause and flattened only
   after ~4.5 s: the wave is scaled by the bands' own sum, so it held while they fell, and a trace a hair off the middle
   straddled two rows. It now goes flat when the music isn't playing (~0.5 s of phosphor fade), drawn rounded. Test:
   `the_scope_goes_flat_when_the_music_pauses`; pty `audio` now checks Singularity's meters and Terminal's osc/fft hold
   still while paused.
10. **security** — two advisories published 2026-10-19 on the TLS chain librespot's HTTP client uses (RUSTSEC-2026-0382
   `rustls-webpki`, -0381 `webpki-roots`: distrusted emSign intermediates still trusted): patched to 0.103.16 / 1.0.10.
11. **resources** (Linux, the owner's machine) — Soloist's cache had grown to **2.8 GB** (136 files, ~20 MB each:
   lossless audio) in four days. Soloist takes `--cache-size <MB>` (min 100; default no limit), which Singularity
   doesn't pass. **Fixed (D61)**: `engine.cache_gb` (default 5 GB, 0 = no limit, on the Settings page) becomes
   `--cache-size`; `update_test.py` checks the Daemon passes it. Tests: `the_cache_size_becomes_soloists_megabytes`,
   `a_schema_3_config_gains_the_cache_size_keeping_its_engine`.
12. **bug** (owner report, 2026-10-23, Ghostty 1.3.1 on Linux) — one wheel notch moved every list three rows: Ghostty
   sends `mouse-scroll-multiplier` reports per notch (`discrete:3` by default). **Fixed**: input is read on its own
   thread and timestamped; same-direction wheel reports within 8 ms of the first are one step (`term::WheelBursts`).
   Test: `a_wheel_burst_is_one_step`; pty `wheel-burst`.
13. **polish, open** (macOS, found by reading) — Soloist in the VM anchors the position at its own `timestamp_ms`,
   taken by the *guest's* clock, and the host extrapolates from it with its own. If the guest clock lags after the Mac
   sleeps (until its NTP client resyncs), the shown position runs ahead by the lag. Not seen yet: check after a sleep
   on the Mac; the fix would measure the guest–host offset when the VM wakes.
14. **bug** (Soloist's supervisor, found reviewing the Engine state machines) — the wait before restarting Soloist
   (2 s, doubling to 60 s) was only reset by an update, so it kept growing for the Daemon's whole life; and "Restart
   the player" and a new API key counted as crashes. After a few, each waited up to a minute, refusing commands
   meanwhile. **Fixed**: asked-for restarts (`Exit::Restart`) go straight back up; a run of 5 minutes or more starts
   the waits again from 2 s (`restart_wait`). Test: `restart_waits_grow_while_failing_and_start_over_after_a_healthy_run`.
15. **resources** (AppEngine) — every command ran `pgrep` synchronously on the async runtime to see whether the app
   still runs: one process per percent of a volume drag. **Fixed**: a check counts for 2 s.

### Checked, fine

- **Secrets**: the Keychain and Secret Service get values over stdin (`security -i` with hex, `secret-tool store`),
  never on a command line. Exception, documented since #2: Soloist only takes its key as `--api-key`, so on Linux it
  shows in `ps` to local users (in the macOS VM, only inside the guest). Inherent to Soloist.
- **Subprocesses**: every `Command::new` uses fixed arguments or passes values as separate argv entries; notification
  text reaches `osascript` as `argv`, not spliced into the script; `pkill -x Spotify`/`spotify` match exact names.
  `secret-tool`, `pactl`, `parec`, `pgrep`, `pkill`, `tar` are found on `PATH` (polish: absolute paths).
- **Terminal output**: all text goes through ratatui's `set_stringn`, which drops control characters, so an escape
  sequence in a track or playlist name can't reach the terminal; the only raw writes are kitty graphics commands built
  from numbers and an OSC 52 copy that is base64.
- **Panics outside tests** in the Daemon and config: the rest are invariants (static regexes, the default config
  parsing, `Mutex` poisoning after another panic, `unreachable!` behind a match on the same value).
- **Protocol, a Client that stops reading**: memory always stayed bounded (events reach each Client through a
  broadcast channel that skips ahead for a slow one), but such a Client was never let go: its tasks stayed blocked for
  as long as it was connected. **Fixed**: everything to a Client, the initial snapshot included, goes through one
  writer with a 5 s deadline per message; a Client that takes nothing for 5 s has its session closed (a real Client
  reconnects by itself). Test: `a_client_that_stops_reading_is_let_go` (fails without the fix).
- **`follow_config`** stats `config.toml` once a second: one tiny syscall a second; acceptable (file watching would
  add a dependency for nothing measurable).
- **macOS socket path** `…/T/singularity-user/…`: `uid()` reads `/proc`, which macOS lacks, so the fallback name says
  "user". Harmless (macOS's `$TMPDIR` is already private to the user); renaming it would strand a running Daemon on the
  old path. Polish.

- **`unsafe`** (Client: kitty shared memory, `proc_name`, `kill`, `getrusage`, libproc; Daemon: libproc, Core Audio,
  Now Playing's run loop, `localtime_r`, `prctl` in `pre_exec`, `fchmod`): buffers sized before every call, counts
  bounds-checked, the shared-memory segment created exclusively (`O_EXCL`, 0600) and unlinked on every failure path
  and after 2 s, `kill` only after checking the pid is a `singularityd`. Fine apart from finding 6. Miri can't run
  FFI, so these stay reviewed by hand.
- **Soloist build downloads** (`update.rs`): only over HTTPS from Spotify's CDN (no signature exists to check; TLS
  to `spotifycdn.com` is the trust, as for a manual download), unpacked into a fresh `soloist.new/` that is removed on
  any failure, and run (`--version`) before it's installed; the old build stays as `soloist.old/`. GNU tar refuses
  `..` and absolute member names. Polish: the body is read whole without a size cap (a build is 35 MB; a cap of a few
  hundred MB would fence a broken CDN answer), and `tar` comes from `PATH`.
- **Migrations**: the listening log from `user_version` 1 → 2 and config schemas 1 → 3 have tests that keep every row
  and every comment; DST and midnight have `tests/stats_tz.rs`.

- **String slicing** in parsers (`&s[a..b]`): every position comes from `find` on the same string or is a fixed
  ASCII text, so no slice can split a character.
- **Timers and polling loops** in the Daemon: each wakes at most 4× a second while idle (the tap waiting for music
  1×/s, the app-tap manager 1×/s, the VM's PCM accept loop 4×/s, `follow_config` 1×/s, librespot's watchdog every
  5 s); the idle Daemon measures ~0 % CPU.
  Linux, measured (2026-10-23, the owner's idle Daemon and Client, `/proc` context switches over 10 s): the Daemon
  ~5.6 wakeups/s and no CPU ticks; the Client (unfocused, 30 fps) ~21 wakeups/s, ~0.7 % CPU. The Client's loops: the
  frame loop, Daemon reconnects (300 ms, then 3 s), the input thread (blocked in `read`, no wakeups).
- **Time**: positions are anchored as (position, Unix ms) and extrapolated with `saturating_sub`, so a clock that steps
  back freezes the position instead of wrapping; Connect cluster positions are moved to the local clock on arrival.
  `Instant` is used for every interval and deadline (`Instant::duration_since` saturates). Backup retention and
  stats use signed day numbers. Exception: finding 13 (the VM's guest clock).
- **Engine state machines** (2026-10-23): Soloist's supervisor (start, pairing, updates at a quiet moment, resume,
  bad key, expiry, the VM) apart from finding 14: every exit path stops the child (`kill_on_drop`, the VM session's
  stdin), a staged-build notice from before a restart is ignored (`update_waiting`), the resume point survives a
  failed restart. librespot: an asked-for restart is immediate, a dropped session restarts after 1 s with the saved
  state, the watchdog catches a session that closed while idle. AppEngine: finding 15; otherwise a pass-through.

### Resources, measured (macOS, release, idle, nothing playing)

The owner's own idle Client: **9–10 % CPU**, 80 MB RSS; its Daemon (librespot) ~0 %, 6 MB. `sample` shows the Client's
main thread mostly waiting; the work is drawing and sending the animated Visuals' frames to the terminal
(`Kitty::transmit`): they animate while nothing plays. In the pty harness (fake Daemon, default layout):

| Client | focused (30 fps) | unfocused (10 fps) |
|--------|------------------|--------------------|
| pixel tier (kitty images) | 5.5 % | 2.3 % |
| cell tier | 2.8 % | 1.2 % |

The terminal's own work decoding those frames comes on top.

**Fixed (D60, owner: "slow sounds good"; undone 2026-10-21 by D62: 5 fps looked choppy, and Linux's costs are small)**: while nothing plays and nothing was touched for 2 s, the Client draws at
most 5 frames a second. Same harness, focused: pixel tier **5.5 → 2.1 %**, cell tier **2.8 → 1.2 %**. What's left is
the cost of one frame (~4 ms in the pixel tier: the whole screen plus the hole's image); worth a look in the frame
profile still to do (a frame that changed nothing could skip the image upload).

### Soak (macOS, 2026-10-20 17:03–22:50, the owner's own Daemon and Client)

A read-only sampler (`~/.cache/singularity/soak.sh`, results in `soak.csv` there; not in git) recorded both processes
every 5 min while the owner used them as usual: 1 h 45 min on the old build, then 4 h on the new one (librespot). The
owner shortened it from 24 h.

| | old build | new build (4 h) |
|-|-----------|-----------------|
| Client memory | 64 MB average, 82 MB max | 42–69 MB, no trend |
| Daemon memory | 6 MB | 9–24 MB, back and forth, no trend (librespot's buffers while playing) |
| Client threads / open files | 14 / 28 | 11–14 / 31–32, flat |
| Daemon threads / open files | 24 / 41 | 24 / 36–45, flat |
| Client CPU, average | — | **9.2 %** (25 min of CPU in 4 h 34 min) |
| Daemon CPU, average | — | 0.8 % (2 min 10 s) |
| `daemon.log` | 0644 | 0600, 32 KB after 4.5 h |

No leak: memory, threads and open files don't climb. The Daemon's CPU time says music played for much of it, so the
Client's 9 % is its **playing** cost in the owner's terminal (Visuals at 30 fps); the harness's idle 1.2 % doesn't
cover that. That is the per-frame cost (~4 ms in the pixel tier) again: the frame profile is the lever, and it
belongs in the budgets. Note: macOS `ps`'s `%cpu` column is a decaying average and showed the Daemon at 0.0 % the
whole time; use cumulative CPU time (`ps -o time`) to measure.

### Linux, measured (2026-10-21, the owner's own Client in kitty, release build, Soloist playing)

`/proc` CPU time in 5 s windows (shares of one core; Ryzen 7 7800X3D): idle 0.5 %; playing, unfocused (10 fps)
**1.2–2 %**; playing, focused (30 fps) **2.2–3.2 %**, plus **3–5.6 %** in kitty decoding the frames (shared memory);
Daemon 0.2 %, 50 MB; Client 73 MB. `perf record` works without call graphs only (the kernel refuses `-g` and DWARF
unwinding at `perf_event_paranoid` 2), and the installed binaries are stripped, so the hot paths came from heaptrack
instead (pty `designs` run, release with symbols): 40 k allocations/s, a third of them the lyrics Pane re-wrapping
every line every frame and ~10 % covers' tint keys; both fixed (`b21d8e6`): **20 k/s**. Peak heap ~10 MB.
The owner saw the osc lag the music: its phosphor persistence was per drawn frame (0.3 s of trail at 10 fps, 0.6 s at
5); now per second (`434112d`). Latency, measured with `pw-top` while playing: Soloist follows the headset's graph
cycle (1024 samples at 48 kHz, 21 ms) and doesn't buffer ahead; the tap captures in 30 ms cycles; the analyser's
window is 46 ms: the frames sit within a few tens of ms of the sound.
